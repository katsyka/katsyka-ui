The quiet craft of writing interface copy

Good microcopy is invisible. People only notice it when it is missing, vague, or trying too hard to be clever.

MOMira Okafor · Oct 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Every label, hint and error is a tiny promise about what will happen next. When the promise is clear, people move quickly. When it is not, they hesitate, and hesitation is where most drop-off begins.

Say what the button does

“Submit” describes the mechanism; “Create account” describes the outcome. Prefer verbs that name the result, and keep them consistent across the product. See our writing guidelines for the full list.

  • Lead with the verb: Save, Send, Delete
  • Match the title of the dialog that opened it
  • Avoid “OK” when the action is destructive
The best error message is the one that never appears; the second best tells you exactly how to fix it.— A designer we agree with

Errors are instructions

Replace “Invalid input” with what is wrong and how to fix it. Press ⌘ Enter to submit is nicer than a bare shortcut, and placing the message next to the field beats a banner at the top.

Note: link the hint to its input with aria-describedby so screen readers announce it.
<input id="email" aria-describedby="email-hint">
<p id="email-hint">We’ll only use this for receipts.</p>
