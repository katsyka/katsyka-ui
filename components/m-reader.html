The ferry that runs on time, every time

By Kari Holm · 11 November

There is a ferry on the west coast that has not been late in nine years. Not because the weather is kind, it isn't, but because the crew decided long ago that a timetable is a promise, and promises are kept in small, unglamorous ways.

Captain Arne Vik starts each morning at 05:10 with the same walk around the deck. He checks the ramps, the lines, the life rafts. "Most delays aren't storms," he says. "They're a loose bolt nobody noticed on Tuesday."

Small margins, every day

The crossing takes 22 minutes. The schedule gives it 25. Those three minutes are the whole secret: never spent, always saved, folded into the next departure when the wind turns.

“A timetable is a promise. You keep it in the boring parts.”

Passengers notice. Commuters set their alarms by it; the café at the terminal times its first batch of buns to the ferry's horn. When a winter storm closed the road last February, the ferry was the only thing still running, on time.

Asked whether he worries about the streak ending, Vik shrugs. "It'll end one day. Then we start counting again."
