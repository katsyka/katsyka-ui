Designing forms people finish

Most form abandonment comes from a handful of avoidable mistakes. Here is how to remove them one by one.

Ask for less

Every field is a question the user has to answer. Before adding one, decide what you will actually do with the answer, and whether you could get it later.

Optional fields deserve particular suspicion: they look free, but they still cost attention.

Defer what you can

Billing address, company size and job title can almost always wait until after the first success moment.

Label everything

Placeholders disappear the moment someone types. A visible label above the field is the cheapest accessibility win available.

Keep labels short and put help text below the field, linked with aria-describedby.

Validate kindly

Validate on blur, not on every keystroke, and never before someone has had a chance to finish. Once a field is wrong, re-check it live so the error clears the instant it is fixed.

Write useful errors

“Invalid input” tells nobody anything. Say what is wrong and how to fix it, in the same place the eye already is.

Make the button honest

“Submit” describes the mechanism. “Create account” or “Send message” describes the outcome. Disable it only while a request is in flight, and say so.

When it succeeds, show what happened and what comes next.
