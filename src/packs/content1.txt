<div class="ar-b" id="b"><p>In January our storage bill passed our payroll for the first time. That was the week we started pricing out racks. This post is the honest version of what happened next, including the parts that made us look silly.</p><h2>The number that started it</h2><p>We stored 40 terabytes of customer uploads, most of which nobody had opened in a year. Object storage is cheap per gigabyte, but egress is not, and every backup, every thumbnail regeneration and every analytics job paid it again.</p><p>We modelled three options: tiering to cold storage, a second cheaper provider, and our own hardware in a colocation space two kilometres from the office. The third looked best on paper by a wide margin, which is exactly when you should distrust paper.</p>
<figure><img src="media/pics/w/1033.jpg" alt="Escalators in an empty station hall"><figcaption>The colocation building's entrance. It used to be a ticket hall.</figcaption></figure>
<h2>What went wrong</h2><p>Twice we lost writes for a few minutes during the cut-over. Both times the cause was the same: a replication check that passed in staging, where latency is near zero, and failed under real load.</p><blockquote><p>Every migration plan is a plan for the happy path. Write the unhappy one first.</p></blockquote><p>The fix was boring. We added a canary that wrote, read and deleted one object every ten seconds and paged us if any step took over 200 ms. It caught the next problem forty minutes before customers would have.</p><h2>Would we do it again?</h2><p>Yes, at this size. Our monthly storage cost fell by 71%. Below about 10 TB, we wouldn't bother: the people-hours eat the savings.</p></div>

<article class="dc2-a" id="a"><p class="mu">Getting started</p><h1>Environment variables</h1><p class="dc2-lead">Keep secrets out of your code. shipit reads variables from your project settings and injects them at build and run time.</p>
<h2 id="s1">Adding a variable</h2><p>Run the command from your project folder. The value is encrypted before it leaves your machine.</p><pre><code>shipit env add DATABASE_URL --env production</code></pre><p>Variables are scoped to an environment. A preview deployment never sees production values unless you copy them explicitly.</p>
<h2 id="s2">Using variables at build time</h2><p>Build-time variables are baked into the output. Only expose values that are safe to ship to browsers, and prefix them with <code>PUBLIC_</code> so it's obvious in reviews.</p><div class="dc2-call">@I(info,18)<p>Changing a build-time variable needs a new deploy. Runtime variables take effect on the next request.</p></div>
<h2 id="s3">Local development</h2><p>Pull the development values into a local file. It's added to <code>.gitignore</code> automatically.</p><pre><code>shipit env pull .env.local</code></pre>
<h2 id="s4">Limits</h2><p>Each project can have 200 variables of up to 64 KB each. Names are case-sensitive and must start with a letter.</p><div class="dc2-pn"><a href="#">@I(left,16)<span><small>Previous</small>Your first deploy</span></a><a href="#" class="nx"><span><small>Next</small>Custom domains</span>@I(right,16)</a></div></article>
