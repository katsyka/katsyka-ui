Reading a city from its power lines

Overhead cables are a map of a city's past arguments. Where they still hang, someone once decided burying them wasn't worth the money, and nobody has reopened that decision since.

In Tokyo, more than 90% of distribution lines are still above ground, a choice made in the 1950s for speed of reconstruction. In Paris, almost none are. The difference isn't wealth: it's which disaster each city prepared for.

Next time you look up, try reading the wires. The thick ones carry power, the thin bundles carry data, and the little cylinders are transformers stepping voltage down for a few dozen homes.
