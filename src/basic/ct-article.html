What we learned moving 40 TB off the cloud

Nine months, two outages and one very large spreadsheet. Here is the cost curve and what we would do differently.

Ines Aldana10 November 2026 · min read

In January our storage bill passed our payroll for the first time. That was the week we started pricing out racks. This post is the honest version of what happened next, including the parts that made us look silly.

The number that started it

We stored 40 terabytes of customer uploads, most of which nobody had opened in a year. Object storage is cheap per gigabyte, but egress is not, and every backup, every thumbnail regeneration and every analytics job paid it again.

We modelled three options: tiering to cold storage, a second cheaper provider, and our own hardware in a colocation space two kilometres from the office. The third looked best on paper by a wide margin, which is exactly when you should distrust paper.

What went wrong

Twice we lost writes for a few minutes during the cut-over. Both times the cause was the same: a replication check that passed in staging, where latency is near zero, and failed under real load.

Every migration plan is a plan for the happy path. Write the unhappy one first.

The fix was boring. We added a canary that wrote, read and deleted one object every ten seconds and paged us if any step took over 200 ms. It caught the next problem forty minutes before customers would have.

Would we do it again?

Yes, at this size. Our monthly storage cost fell by 71%. Below about 10 TB, we wouldn't bother: the people-hours eat the savings.
